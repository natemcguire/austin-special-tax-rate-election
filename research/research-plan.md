# Tax Expertise Research Plan

## Goal

Build a maintained body of U.S. state-and-local tax knowledge with deep coverage of
property taxes in Texas/Austin and Virginia/Fairfax County. Each geography has its
own publishing track and domain. The corpus supports public explainers,
year-over-year archives, and transparent calculators; it is not individualized tax
or legal advice.

## STORM research passes

Run separate, narrowly named topics so sources and claims remain attributable:

1. **United States property tax framework** — constitutional allocation, state and
   local revenue systems, assessment concepts, levy/rate terminology, exemptions,
   limits, appeals, school finance, and the federal SALT interaction.
2. **Texas property tax system 2026** — Texas Constitution and Tax Code, appraisal
   districts, ARBs and protests, Truth-in-Taxation, no-new-revenue and voter-approval
   rates, homestead rules and caps, school compression, debt rates, elections, and
   2025–2026 legislative changes.
3. **Austin property tax stack 2026** — City of Austin, Travis County, Austin ISD,
   ACC, Central Health, special districts, TCAD values, exemptions, adopted and
   proposed rates, budgets, bonds, Project Connect, and Proposition Q history.
4. **Virginia real property tax system 2026** — state constitutional/statutory
   authority, local assessment and equalization, classifications, relief, appeals,
   levies, public hearings, and state/local fiscal limits.
5. **Fairfax County property taxes FY2027** — real-estate assessments, Board of
   Supervisors rate adoption, FCPS budget relationship, tax relief, appeals,
   stormwater/service districts, personal-property tax as a separate system, and
   year-over-year taxpayer impacts.

Each pass performs research and outline generation. Generated articles are retained
only as leads; they are not publication candidates.

## Primary-source hierarchy

1. Constitutions, statutes, administrative codes, session laws, and official legal
   guidance.
2. Comptrollers, departments of taxation, appraisal/assessment offices, adopted
   budgets, rate orders, audited financial reports, and certified election results.
3. Legislative fiscal notes, official manuals, public datasets, meeting records, and
   government FAQs.
4. Academic and nonpartisan institutional analysis for comparison or interpretation.
5. Local reporting for chronology and stakeholder context.

Campaign sites, opinion articles, search snippets, and STORM prose can suggest
questions but cannot establish a public factual claim.

## Evidence ledger

Every publishable claim records:

- jurisdiction and taxing unit;
- tax type and topic;
- claim or numeric value with units;
- tax year, fiscal year, effective date, and status (`proposed`, `adopted`, `certified`);
- primary source URL plus statute section, page, table, or meeting item;
- secondary corroboration when useful;
- verification date and reviewer note;
- superseded-by link when a later rate, budget, or law replaces it.

## Public guides

### Austin/Texas

- How an Austin property-tax bill is calculated across overlapping taxing units.
- Appraisal, homestead exemptions and caps, protests, ARB appeals, and deadlines.
- No-new-revenue versus voter-approval rates and when an election is required.
- City budget and rate chronology from FY 2024-25 forward, including Proposition Q.
- A homeowner scenario calculator that exposes every input and never substitutes for
  an official tax statement.

### Fairfax/Virginia

- Assessment versus tax rate, annual equalization, and the Board's adoption process.
- Fairfax real-estate relief and appeal pathways with current deadlines.
- County, FCPS, stormwater, and service-district relationships.
- A separate explanation for Virginia personal-property/car tax to avoid conflating
  it with real property tax.
- A Fairfax-only site on its own future domain. Cross-jurisdiction comparison drafts
  remain unpublished research unless both geographic properties later link to a
  clearly labeled comparison resource.

## Publication gates

- No claim is published from STORM alone.
- Two-source verification is required for election outcomes, disputed figures, and
  calculations; at least one source must be primary.
- Calculators receive boundary tests, official-example reconciliation, visible
  assumptions, rounding rules, and a last-verified date.
- Proposed values are visually distinct and automatically removed from “current”
  summaries after adoption.
- A quarterly source check and annual post-adoption refresh are required for each
  jurisdiction.
