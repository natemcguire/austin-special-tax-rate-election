Texas and Virginia

Austin ≠ Fairfax

The same words—assessment, rate, relief—operate under materially different state and local systems.

Side-by-side

QuestionAustin / TexasFairfax / Virginia
Who sets value?County appraisal districtFairfax Department of Tax Administration
General valuation ruleMarket value, subject to qualifying statutory appraisal limitsFair market value under Virginia Constitution
General owner-occupied capQualifying continuing homestead: generally 10% appraised-value limit plus new improvementsNo general percentage limit on annual assessment change
2026 headline rateMultiple overlapping units; no single Austin total rate$1.12 County base per $100, plus parcel-specific districts or charges
Appeal institutionAppraisal district / ARB, then applicable appeal pathDTA, independent Board of Equalization, or court
Senior/disability reliefState and local exemptions, including school exemptionsCounty income/asset-tested relief and deferral programs
Do not compare headline rates alone. Taxable-value definitions, exemptions, assessment limits, taxing-unit stacks, and special districts differ.

Fairfax 2026 snapshot

The adopted base real-estate rate is $1.12 per $100. Fairfax reports 2026 mean assessments of $1,012,504 for single-family homes, $612,580 for townhomes, and $387,560 for condominiums. These means are not estimates for a particular parcel.

The 2026 administrative and Board of Equalization deadlines—April 1 and June 1—have passed. New deadlines must be verified for each assessment year.
