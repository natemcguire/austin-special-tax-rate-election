# Austin property tax: a resident's guide

Research draft — verified 2026-08-01. This is general information, not tax or
legal advice. Your appraisal notice and official tax statement control.

## The shortest useful explanation

An Austin property-tax bill is not one City tax. The appraisal district determines
value; each overlapping taxing unit applies its own exemptions and rate; the tax
office collects the resulting levies. A simplified line-item calculation is:

`taxable value for that unit × rate ÷ 100 = that unit's tax`

Add the applicable units to estimate the total. Do not apply one exemption or one
cap indiscriminately across the entire bill.

## Value, exemptions, and the homestead limit

Texas does not levy a state property tax. A qualifying residence homestead receives
a $140,000 school-district exemption in 2026, with an additional $60,000 school
exemption for a qualifying owner age 65 or older or disabled. Other taxing units
may offer different exemptions.

For a continuing qualifying homestead, appraised value generally cannot rise more
than 10% per year plus new improvements and cannot exceed market value. That does
not cap the bill at 10%: rates, exemptions, new improvements, and overlapping units
can change.

## Rate language that is easy to confuse

- **No-new-revenue rate:** a calculated benchmark designed to raise roughly the
  same levy from property taxed in both years, after statutory adjustments.
- **Voter-approval rate:** a calculated threshold under Texas law. The formula and
  election consequence depend on the type of taxing unit and statutory adjustments.
- **Adopted rate:** the rate actually voted on by the governing body.
- **Effective rate after an election:** when an adopted increase is contingent on
  voter approval, the election outcome can change the rate that may take effect.

## Check your own property

1. Start with the current appraisal notice and confirm ownership, characteristics,
   exemptions, market value, and appraised value.
2. Use the official truth-in-taxation database or tax statement to identify every
   unit that applies to the parcel.
3. Calculate each unit separately using that unit's taxable value and adopted rate.
4. Treat a calculator result as an estimate until rates are adopted and the tax
   office issues the statement.
