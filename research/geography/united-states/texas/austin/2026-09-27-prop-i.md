# Proposition I — editorial and source record

Verified September 27, 2026. Primary-source-first independent coverage; no endorsement inferred.

## Sources and interpretation

- [Official revised election notice](https://services.austintexas.gov/edims/document.cfm?id=481839), PDF page 4: confirms Proposition **I**, charter question and November 3 election. Homepage paraphrases the question and links to the original.
- [Adopted ordinance 20260226-050](https://services.austintexas.gov/edims/document.cfm?id=468615): recurring assessment program. Use **at least three years between cycles**, not an exact three-year mandate. Amendment/repeal needs **nine** council votes; an earlier draft and some reporting omit or misstate that protection.
- [Full petition amendment](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/6029b033d6914a529eedb8b9/t/691feeec9f17e963e3cc995f/1763700460163/PETITION-112125-Independent-Affordability-and-Efficiency-Audit.pdf): review every five years, and completed **within one year before** the specified maintenance-and-operations tax election. Do not say it starts a year before, applies to every annual tax-rate increase, guarantees savings, or automatically implements all recommendations.
- [Austin Current comparison](https://austincurrent.org/2026/07/15/austin-save-austin-now-audit-ordinance-election/): context and attributed concerns about overlap and savings requirements. Underlying documents control when summaries differ.
- [Save Austin Now](https://www.saveaustinnow.com/): explicitly labeled supporting campaign, not a neutral authority.
- [City Clerk election resources](https://www.austintexas.gov/clerk/november-2026-election): early voting October 19–30; Election Day November 3. Readers directed to county-specific eligibility, hours and locations.

## Preservation and design

New primary homepage focuses on the current measure. All Prop Q campaign pages, certified 2025 record, letters and graphics remain. The prior homepage is preserved as `/archive-2026-08-01.html` with a historical banner; its proposed figures deliberately remain as published. After feedback that the restrained editorial design lost the original personality, the design was revised: bold poster typography, coral/yellow/blue accents, a taped Prop I placard, skeptical Austin copy, and the original No Thank Q sign imagery in the archive section. Equal-weight yes/no and supporter/critic sections, citations and archive navigation remain. No party affiliation or new endorsement is invented.
