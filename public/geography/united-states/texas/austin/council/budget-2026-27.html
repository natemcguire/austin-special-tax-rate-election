Every figure carries a status so a proposal can’t quietly become “current,” and so nobody can say the increase was smaller than it was.

Adopted August 12, 2026

What it costs

$6.6 billion citywide, including a $1.5 billion General Fund. That is roughly $300 million more than last year’s amended budget, the one Council had to cut after Prop Q failed. The adopted City rate is $0.579948 per $100 taxable value, the maximum allowed without an election and higher than the $0.574017 voters rejected. The City’s own typical-homeowner example: $113.76 more in property tax, plus $172.08 more in rates and fees, for $285.84 a year.

The budget vote was 9–2. The separate tax-rate vote was 8–2, with one member off the dais. See who voted for it.

Our position: vote YES on Prop I. This budget went up $300 million one year after voters said no. Prop I requires an independent efficiency audit, in the charter, before Council can call another tax-rate election. The numbers and the ballot language.
