Who voted for the increase

A budget vote, a tax-rate vote, and an election-order vote are different actions. We keep them separate so nobody can hide behind “I only voted for the budget.”

FY 2026–27 · August 12, 2026

Nine members voted for a $6.6 billion budget. Eight voted for a $0.579948 City tax rate, higher than the $0.574017 voters rejected nine months earlier. Mike Siegel was off the dais for the rate vote. Kirk Watson and Marc Duchen voted against both.

The July 30 maximum-rate notice was an earlier procedural step, not the final adoption. It passed the same day Council advanced the Prop I charter item.

What this means for Prop I. Eight of these members took the full increase available without an election. The two who voted no, Watson and Duchen, are also the two who have raised concerns about Prop I, on the grounds that Council’s own audit ordinance is enough. That ordinance has no requirement to audit before a tax election and can be repealed with nine votes. Look at the table above and count to nine. Vote YES on Prop I. Read the case.
