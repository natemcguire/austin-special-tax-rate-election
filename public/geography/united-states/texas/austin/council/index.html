Nine votes. One rate. No election.

Austin voters rejected a 57.40¢ City tax rate in November 2025. Nine months later, Council adopted 57.99¢. Higher than what we voted down, and this time nobody asked us. Here is the record, name by name.

Live budget cycle

What Council did

On August 12, Council adopted a $6.6 billion FY 2026–27 budget, about $300 million more than last year’s amended budget, and a $0.579948 City property-tax rate. The budget passed 9–2. The separate tax-rate vote passed 8–2, with one member off the dais. The rate is the maximum the City can adopt without an election, and it is above the $0.574017 that voters rejected in Proposition Q.

How it went

November 4, 2025

Voters reject Prop Q

63.47% vote against a 57.40¢ rate. Council amends the budget down to the 52.40¢ voter-approval rate.

July 16, 2026

Manager proposes the new maximum

$6.6 billion, with a $0.57953 City rate. The new voter-approval rate came out above the number voters had just rejected, so no election is needed to take it.

July 30

Council advances the max rate and an audit on the same day

Council approves the maximum proposed rate for notice, approves a $5.23 million efficiency-study contract, and sends a charter amendment (now Prop I) to the November ballot requiring an audit before future tax-rate elections. Note the order: rate first, audit later.

August 12

Budget and tax rate adopted

Separate votes: 9–2 on the budget, 8–2 on the rate. The City’s own typical-homeowner example goes up $113.76 in property tax plus $172.08 in rates and fees.
