02 / WHAT PROP I DOES

The ballot question,
in plain English.

Prop I does not set a tax rate. It sets a rule: before City Hall asks voters for a tax-rate increase, an outside firm has to go through the books first.

A YES VOTE

Put the audit
in the city charter.

Creates an Independent Affordability and Efficiency Initiative. An outside firm reviews City operations every five years, and a review must be completed within the year before any maintenance-and-operations tax-rate election. The contractor has to commit to finding savings that exceed its own fee. Council cannot repeal it without going back to voters.

A NO VOTE

Trust Council’s
own ordinance.

Rejects the charter amendment. You are left with the ordinance Council passed for itself in February 2026: reviews at least three years apart, no requirement to audit before a tax election, and Council can amend or repeal it with nine votes. No voters involved.

Basis: official election notice, Proposition I, Council’s ordinance 20260226-050, and the full petition text. The ballot summary is shorter than the amendment. Read the amendment.

04 / STRAIGHT ANSWERS

The questions
people actually ask.

Does Prop I lower my tax bill?

Not by itself, and we are not going to pretend otherwise. Prop I does not set a rate. What it does is make the next tax-rate election harder to call without an outside audit on the table first. Lower bills come from Council spending less. This is how you make them look. Read the ballot notice.

Isn’t the City already doing an outside review?

Council passed an ordinance in February 2026, after the petition was already circulating. It has no requirement to audit before a tax election, and nine council votes can repeal it. If Prop I fails, that ordinance stays. If Prop I passes, the requirement goes into the charter where Council cannot touch it without asking you. See the comparison.

Won’t two audit requirements waste money?

Austin Current reported the City could end up running two processes. Council can fix that in an afternoon by aligning its ordinance with the charter. A city with a $6.6 billion budget complaining about the cost of one outside review is not a serious objection. Read the reporting.

Wait, how did the rate go up without a vote?

State law lets the City adopt up to the “voter-approval rate” each year without an election. That rate is recalculated annually, and in 2026 it came out at 57.99¢, above the 57.40¢ voters rejected in 2025. Council took all of it, 8 to 2. Legal? Yes. The message voters sent last November? Ignored. See the FY 2026-27 record.

How does this connect to Prop Q?

Prop Q asked for five cents above the cap. 109,375 Austinites said no, 62,952 said yes. This site said the next step was an independent efficiency audit. Prop I is that step, with the added rule that it happens before City Hall comes back for more. Read the certified 2025 record.
